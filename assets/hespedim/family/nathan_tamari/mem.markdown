One thing that stands out from my trips to Israel is how welcoming and hospitable you and Amit <br>always were, even from a young age.<br>
Our first memory together that I can remember, we played soccer for hours. <br>
We were maybe 7, and neither of us could really speak to each other, besides a couple of words here <br>and there that we knew in each other’s language.<br>
Despite this and despite how you were shy about speaking English, you still made such an effort to <br>make me feel welcome. <br>
Years later, for my bar mitzvah trip to Israel, you invited me out and introduced me to all of your <br>friends. <br>
I remember writing about this day in English class for an essay that year, when your friends had <br>rented out the rooftop of a building and played soccer until midnight. <br>
In the essay I mentioned how surprised I was that you and all of your friends were so caring and<br> interested in my life. <br>
How everyone asked me about America and told me about some distant American relative they had to <br>relate to me.<br>
How you guys taught me slang and translated everything that I didn’t understand. <br>
Most importantly, how comfortable everyone made me feel despite being otherwise alone overseas.<br> When I would come home after summers in Israel, I would be almost upset that my friends and I weren’t as adventurous and spontaneous. <br>
I don’t think any of us will ever heal from your passing. We will never stop telling your stories. <br>
I remember I said I wanted to go running with you one day, and all your friends advised me against <br>it because of all the near-death experiences they had trying to keep up with you. <br>
Your discipline, your good manners and light hearted attitude inspired us all to be better people. <br>
Our last WhatsApp message I was trying to convince you to come to America after your service.<br>
It breaks my heart that we were robbed of this chance. <br>
Until we meet again brother. <br>
We love you forever<br>
